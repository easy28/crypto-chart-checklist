The 25-Point Crypto Chart Checklist

Most bad trades are visible before the entry — they hide in the chart. Run this list in order; it takes about five minutes and every tool it points to is free and needs no signup.

1 · Setup (30 seconds)

  1. Use a real-time chart, not a delayed snapshot. Crypto moves in seconds; open a live crypto chart hub and confirm the last candle is updating.
  2. Pick the right asset page. Start from a clean BTC live chart or ETH live chart, then branch out to the SOL, XRP, DOGE, or ADA chart you actually trade.
  3. Check the candle interval matches your horizon. Scalpers need 1–15m; swing traders need 4h–1D. Don't read a daily bias off a 5-minute chart.
  4. Save your watchlist. Keep the 10–20 assets you follow in one place — a crypto watchlist beats 20 browser tabs.
  5. Set alerts instead of staring. Put price alarms on key levels with a free crypto price alert tool and walk away from the screen.

2 · Trend & structure (the honest part)

  1. Name the trend in one sentence. If you can't say "higher highs, higher lows" (or its inverse), you don't have a trend — you have chop.
  2. Find the last clean break of structure. Where did price last take out a swing high/low? That level, not your P&L, defines the narrative.
  3. Check whether the move is running out of room. Exhaustion, doji clusters, and shrinking range candles matter more near the end of a leg. Learn the tells from a complete candlestick pattern guide.
  4. Look left for untouched levels. Prior consolidation zones and old wicks act like magnets; entries into open air are the ones that get wicked out.
  5. Zoom out once per session. What looks like a crash on the 15m is often a pullback on the weekly. Compare against the LINK, XMR, or DOT weekly before concluding anything.

3 · Momentum & indicators (confluence, not clutter)

  1. Use at most three indicators. Two trend filters + one momentum gauge is plenty. Everything else is decoration.
  2. Check divergence on the pullback. New price high with a weaker oscillator high is the cheapest warning you'll ever get.
  3. Prefer indicators you can explain. If you can't describe what the line measures, delete it. Browse purpose-built crypto indicators instead of stacking defaults.
  4. Try a rule-based dip-buy signal. The two-line pullback rule in the Trend Dip indicator design walks forward far better than "buy the red candle".
  5. Watch capitulation behavior at lows. Panic volume plus a stalled downtrend is the setup the Capitulation Reversal indicator was designed to catch.
  6. Don't trade the first signal alone. Wait for the second touch, the retest, or the divergence confluence. First prints lie.

4 · Risk before entry (non-negotiable)

  1. Define the invalidation level first. If you can't say where the idea is wrong, it's not an idea — it's a hope.
  2. Size the position from the stop. Risk a fixed fraction, not a round number of dollars. The formulas live in this position sizing & stop-loss guide.
  3. Target at least 1.5R. If the nearest obstacle is closer than 1.5× your stop distance, skip the trade. The boring market scanner exists precisely to filter these out.
  4. Check the funding / sentiment backdrop. Crowd positioning is fuel — see what it says via market sentiment analysis before you join it.
  5. Plan the partial exit. Decide what you'll trim at 1R before the candle closes green and your plan changes for you.

5 · Backtest before you trust anything

  1. Run the strategy on history, not vibes. Any rule you just invented deserves a lap through free crypto backtesting before real money.
  2. Stress the parameters. If profit only exists at one exact lookback length, it's curve-fit noise — sweep the settings, don't accept the first result.
  3. Let a generator propose, then you veto. Describe a setup, get a draft strategy from the AI strategy generator, and judge it with the same backtest bar as everything else.
  4. Pit your rules against others. Watching how a strategy ranks in the strategy arena is a cheap reality check on survivorship bias.

6 · After the entry

  1. Log the checklist result, not just the P&L. "Passed 22/25, still entered" is the most honest line in any trading journal.
  2. Track the portfolio as a whole. Correlated bags behave like one big position — keep them visible in a single crypto portfolio tracker.
  3. Automate the boring monitoring. Alerts, watchlists, and scheduled scans are free; attention isn't.
  4. Re-run this page monthly. Markets drift, and so do habits. New to all of this? Start with the beginner's guide and the DCA playbook first.
